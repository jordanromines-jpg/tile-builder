# Competitors and landscape

Research for Tile Builder, 2 Oct 2026.

**How this was done, and its limits.** All facts come from web search. This session's network blocked every product
site, app store page and review site (everpieces.com, magniko.app, tilbo.app, apps.apple.com, lego.com, brickit.app,
rebrickable.com and others), so no page was read directly. Each fact below comes from the search engine's extract of
the page cited. Numbers are as the extracts gave them on 2 Oct 2026. The session's search budget ran out before
Brickit, Rebrickable and the deeper LEGO review themes were researched. Those sections say so. "Not confirmed" means
the search found nothing either way. It does not mean the feature is missing.

## The short version

- **EverPieces: Magnetic Tiles is the closest competitor, and it already does most of what Tile Builder plans.** It
  has 100+ guided 3D builds, a 3D model you can turn, a piece list, build stages, a private photo album, no ads, no
  accounts and support for any brand [1]. Its free web guides also come up in search results for build ideas [3].
- **Two more tile apps say they match builds to what you own.** Magniko keeps your inventory and "surfaces only the
  designs you can build right now" [7]. Tilbo (not yet released) offers a daily build "matched to your child's age and
  the tiles you have at home", with a photo and a sticker at the end [8]. So "you can build it!" is not new on its own.
- **No competitor was found that reads steps aloud for children who can't read yet, shows missing tiles as pictures,
  suggests swaps, or handles each brand's tall triangle.** All of these are "not confirmed" rather than "absent".
- **The brands themselves sell guided builds on paper.** Connetix PRO tins each hold four guided designs before free
  building [17][18]. PicassoTiles sells idea books that list the pieces each build needs and rate its difficulty
  [24][25]. Magna-Tiles says on purpose that it includes no instructions [11].
- **Free printables are plentiful.** One site alone offers 300 ideas [27]. Their weakness is that they don't know which
  tiles a family owns. A popular eBook works around this by keeping every build under 60 tiles [35].

## 1. EverPieces (everpieces.com)

### What it is

"EverPieces: Magnetic Tiles is an iPhone and iPad guide for magnetic tile builds." You "choose from 100+ 3D models,
check the pieces, rotate the model, and follow each build stage" [1]. The company describes its apps as helping
"parents and children turn the pieces they already own into shared moments of imagining, problem-solving, and play".
Each app gives "just enough 3D guidance to get started, then leaving room for hands-on building together" [2].

### Platforms

- An iPhone and iPad app on the App Store [1].
- Every build also has a web guide. You can "open a complete guide in the browser, gather the exact tiles, and follow
  the visual stages together" [1]. The carrot page is an example [3].
- Android: not confirmed. Mac: not confirmed.

### Price

- "30+ complete free builds" and "no ads" [1].
- The builds are grouped into "four imaginative Worlds". "Little Wonders is free, and additional Worlds are optional
  one-time purchases that can be restored through the App Store." There is no subscription [1].
- What each tile World costs: **not confirmed**. For comparison, the sister Train Tracks app has 5 free layouts and a
  "one-time $19.99 full library purchase" that unlocks 43 more [4].

### Library size and organisation

- 100+ builds in four Worlds. Two World names were found: Little Wonders (free) and Bloom Garden [1]. The other two:
  not confirmed.
- The page says families can "find a build that matches the time, tiles, and curiosity your family has today" [1].
- Example builds [1][3]:

| Build | World | Level | Pieces | Stages | Age | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rainbow Flower | Bloom Garden | Standard | 59 | 5 | not confirmed | not confirmed |
| Jeep | not confirmed | Standard | 13 | 3 | not confirmed | not confirmed |
| Helicopter | not confirmed | Standard | 22 | 5 | not confirmed | not confirmed |
| Carrot | not confirmed | Standard | 26 (16 squares, 6 equilateral triangles, 4 isosceles triangles) | 4 | 5+ | about 25 min |

- Other levels besides "Standard": not confirmed.

### How a build is presented

- "A rotatable finished model with an exact tile count" [1].
- "Guided build stages that isolate the tiles to place next" [1]. A build has 3 to 5 stages in the examples above,
  so a stage adds several tiles at once.
- A piece list by shape and count. "Colors are flexible — the shapes and quantities are the useful part" [3].
- At the end you mark the model complete [1].
- Read-aloud steps: not confirmed. Pictures-only steps for children who can't read: not confirmed.

### Does it know what tiles a family owns?

**Not confirmed.** The site says "each model shows the shapes and quantities it needs" and "families can choose builds
that fit the pieces they have" [1]. That reads as the parent comparing the piece list with their own tiles. No search
result described an inventory screen, a filter by owned pieces, or a "you need 2 more" message.

### Brands

"Brand-independent" and uses "common full-size magnetic tile shapes"; colours do not have to match [1]. Whether it
accounts for the different tall-triangle lengths of each brand: not confirmed. Brand-only pieces such as Connetix
rectangles, windows and doors: not confirmed (the phrase "common shapes" suggests not).

### Photo album and "optional AI art"

- "Mark a model complete and save an optional photo to see everything your child has built in one private album" [1].
- AI art is "parent-controlled and can be skipped". You "choose a look after the build is finished" [1].
- "You can finish a build without a photo, account, or AI. If you choose AI art, EverPieces uploads a temporary photo
  copy only after you review and accept the explanation" [1].

### Privacy

"Photos and progress stay private, with no profiles, comments, or social feed" [1]. No account is needed [1]. It is
not clear whether "no profiles" also means there are no separate profiles for each child. Offline use: not confirmed.
App Store privacy label: not confirmed.

### Children and parents

The copy speaks to parents: "builds to make with your child" [1]. The one parent-only control found is AI art [1].
A separate kid mode, a grown-ups check, or kid profiles: not confirmed.

### Look and feel

Not confirmed beyond the names (Worlds, "Little Wonders", "Bloom Garden") and the copy's calm, family tone [1][2].
No screenshots could be viewed.

### App Store rating and reviews

**Not confirmed.** Search did not return the App Store listing, and the App Store was blocked. No reviews, Reddit
threads or press coverage were found.

### Who makes it and when it launched

A search extract said the site footer names **Senkor L.L.C.** [2]. Nothing else was found about the company. Launch
date: not confirmed.

### Sister apps

- **EverPieces: Train Tracks.** 48 guided 3D layouts for wooden track with exact piece lists, and a "closed-loop
  check so the track is ready for a train". 5 free, $19.99 one-time unlock [4].
- **EverPieces: Magnetic Sticks.** 92 guided 3D stick-and-ball builds, 22 free, the rest unlocked by a one-time App
  Store purchase (price not confirmed) [5][6].

The train app's "closed-loop check" shows that EverPieces already checks designs by machine where it matters. Whether
it checks tile geometry the way Tile Builder's checker will: not confirmed.

## 2. Other magnet-tile apps found

### Magniko (magniko.app)

- "Tell it which magnetic pieces you own and instantly discover designs you can actually build" [7].
- "Keeps your inventory so it knows exactly what you can build with" and "surfaces only the designs you can build
  right now — no wishful browsing" [7].
- Steps: "every new piece is highlighted so you always know exactly what to place next". The new piece is outlined in
  red, and you can step one piece at a time or see the whole sequence [7].
- Designs come from a community: "browse designs shared by the community and instantly see which ones fit the pieces
  in your collection", and you can share your own builds [7].
- "Free to download for iOS & Android" [7].
- Not confirmed: whether the steps are 3D, which brands and shapes it knows, ages, accounts, in-app purchases, offline
  use, how many designs it has, and how community designs are checked. Its focus on sharing suggests accounts.
- Note: showing *only* designs you can build means a child never sees a near miss ("need 2 more").

### Tilbo (tilbo.app), not yet released

- "Turns magnetic tiles into your daily moment together." Open it and "there's already a perfectly-sized build
  waiting — matched to your child's age and the tiles you have at home" [8].
- Three steps: see today's build "in five seconds" or browse an idea library "by theme, difficulty, and tile count";
  "follow Tilbo through each step on a calm, full-screen canvas" where "you guide and your little one places the
  tiles"; then "snap a photo, earn a sticker, and watch your streak and skill map grow" [8].
- Each build is tagged with a skill: rotation, symmetry, geometry, patterns [8].
- "Privacy-first": age ranges, "never exact birthdays"; "gentle streaks"; English and Chinese [8].
- "Coming soon to iPhone, iPad & Mac" [8].
- Not confirmed: price, brands, 3D or flat drawings, offline use, launch date.

### Virtual magnet games (not about real tiles)

- **Magnet World – Build by Number** (Vector Labs Limited): a 3D "build by number" colouring puzzle, rated 4.6 from
  176 ratings, age 4+ [9].
- **Magformers: Magnetic Balls**: a puzzle game where you drag shapes into place on a 3D model. Locked models open by
  watching ads or paying for premium. Rated 4.2 from 47 ratings, age 4+ [10]. A search extract named the developer as
  Vector Labs Limited, which suggests it is not made by the Magformers brand. An official Magformers app: not found.

These games compete for screen time, not for building with real tiles.

## 3. What the brands provide

### Magna-Tiles

- "MAGNA-TILES are designed for open-ended play, so we don't include instruction manuals" [11].
- The blog publishes building ideas with piece counts and a difficulty level: house, rainbow, bridge, stegosaurus,
  velociraptor, dog, snail, butterfly, robot, ball run, skyscraper [12][13][14]. A beginner butterfly uses 12 squares
  and 6 equilateral triangles [13][14]. One search extract said some of these ideas are written for the Classic
  32-Piece Set [13] (medium confidence).
- Free printables: the Magna-Tales Activity Center [15] and a month-long literacy calendar with a "Read and Build"
  activity each Friday, for home, classroom or small groups [16].
- An official Magna-Tiles app: none found.

### Connetix

- **PRO Build It 4 Ways**: "four collectible tins, each with four guided designs to master before engineering
  extraordinary original builds" [17]. The packs are Animals (30 pieces: elephant, peacock, squirrel, cat), Creatures
  (36: scorpion, wasp, crab, dragonfly), Vehicles (30: digger, helicopter, jet ski, plane) and Flowers (21: tulip,
  rose, daisy, orchid) [18].
- They use "smaller precision PRO tiles", the tin doubles as a build base, and the four guided builds are "a starting
  point before encouraging children to modify, expand or redesign" [18]. They are aimed at "older children and
  advanced builders" [18]. The PRO range is sold as for kids 8+ [19]. They launched in a September; the year was not
  confirmed.
- PRO tiles are smaller than the standard 3-inch tile, so PRO designs are not drop-in projects for Tile Builder.
- Each pack contains an illustrated inspiration booklet of build ideas [21][22].
- Free printable cards (alphabet, numbers, shapes, and themes such as space, fantasy, nature and dinosaurs) and a free
  "Connetix Club" [20].
- A large Pinterest board of ideas [23]. A Connetix app: none found.

### PicassoTiles

- **IDEA Book**: 110 full-colour pages, 150+ ideas "from 2D alphabets to 3D creative buildings", an "easy to advance
  rating scale" and "intuitive directions showing which pieces are needed" [24].
- **STEAM Idea Book**: 230 pages, 300+ builds, a difficulty rating, for use "independently or during parent-child
  playtime" [25]. There is also a Kindle eBook with 300+ ideas [26].
- A PicassoTiles app: none found.

### What the brands tell us

Two brands already pair a guided build with a piece list or a known set of tiles (Connetix tins, PicassoTiles books).
Connetix and EverPieces both frame guidance as a starting point before free play [2][17]. Magna-Tiles doesn't include
instructions at all [11]. Tile Builder should expect some parents to share that view.

## 4. Free printables, books and social media

- **Adventure in a Box**: "300 Creative Magna Tile Ideas" [27]; a pack of 320+ printable cards (letters, numbers,
  shapes, 2D and 3D designs, animals, seasonal) sold as PDFs you can "print and cut as many copies as you want" [28];
  30 architecture cards, each a real building from around the world [29]; 200 printable designs [30]; free number
  challenge cards [31].
- Card packs are also sold on Teachers Pay Teachers [32] and Etsy [33]. Free lists: Saving Talents [34], Two Mama Bears
  [54], Mommy Poppins (50 games and ideas) [53], Babycoo (100 play ideas) [55].
- **Panda Mommy Teacher** (a magnet-tile blog): the "50 Ultimate Magnetic Tile Build Ideas" eBook has 450 pages and
  1,200+ photos, and every build uses "fewer than 60 tiles from any magnetic tile brand" [35]. Her print book
  "Magnetic Tiles Unlimited" has step-by-step picture instructions for 65 builds [36]. An earlier book is "Magnetic
  Tiles Creations for Kids" [37]. The eBook is also sold on Etsy [56].
- **Pinterest, TikTok and YouTube**: idea boards with hundreds of pins [23][38], TikTok searches for single builds such
  as "magnetic tile helicopter" [39], and brand shorts such as a Connetix ball run [40].
- **Libraries**: a public library lends Magna-Tiles "Discovery Kits" with printed guides [41]; another library has a
  Magna-Tiles maker kit guide as a PDF [42].

**How parents use them.** The evidence is indirect. Printables are printed and reused [28]. Books list the pieces
each build needs [24] or cap builds at a tile count most sets can meet [35]. Libraries and classrooms use printed
guides [16][41]. None of these know what a particular family owns. A parent has to count tiles against a list, and a
child who can't read can't use most of them without help.

## 5. Apps that match a physical toy collection to builds

These are the main analogues for "what can I build with what I have".

### Magniko and Tilbo (magnet tiles)

See section 2. Magniko keeps a saved inventory and hides designs you can't build [7]. Tilbo picks a build for the
tiles you have and lets you browse by tile count [8]. Neither was found to show what is missing or suggest swaps.

### Brickit (LEGO): not researched in this session

The search budget ran out and brickit.app was blocked. **Everything in this paragraph is background knowledge, not
confirmed in this session:** Brickit asks you to spread your loose LEGO on a surface and photograph it. It recognises
the bricks and suggests models you can build from them, with steps, and it can show where each needed brick is in the
photo. Price, platforms, accuracy and ratings: not confirmed. To check: brickit.app and its App Store listing.

### Rebrickable (LEGO): not researched in this session

The same limits apply. **Background knowledge, not confirmed in this session:** Rebrickable lets you record the sets
and loose parts you own. Its "build" feature compares them with official sets and fan-designed models ("MOCs") and
shows how much of each you already have, with options to ignore colour or allow substitute parts. Details and
pricing: not confirmed. To check: rebrickable.com help pages.

### What these analogues show (to be confirmed)

They show three ways to answer "what can I build?": a camera scan (Brickit), a typed inventory with a percentage
match (Rebrickable), and a typed inventory that hides what you can't build (Magniko [7]). Tile Builder's plan sits
between the last two: a typed inventory, a yes/no badge, the number of missing tiles, and swaps. With 3 to 6 shapes
instead of thousands of LEGO parts, typing counts is easy and a camera scan is unnecessary.

## 6. LEGO Builder and LEGO DUPLO

### LEGO Builder: 3D Instructions

- LEGO's official app for set instructions. It shows the model in 3D; you can zoom and rotate it "to get the exact
  angle you need" [43][45]. You can search by set name or number, or scan the QR code on the paper instructions to open
  them in the app [43][44].
- **Build Together**: you can "split the build into parts, share a PIN to join, take turns on steps, and watch the
  model come together as a group". Each builder needs their own phone or tablet [46]. A LEGO fan blog called it "an
  excellent tool for families to enjoy LEGO together" [46].
- You can save your sets and keep their instructions in one place, with sets going back to 2000 [45][52].
- It launched in January 2020 as "LEGO Building Instructions" [49]; the Android package is still named
  `legobuildinginstructions` [45]. It is free to download (iOS and Android) [45][47][57].
- Whether each step shows a parts callout: not confirmed in this session.
- **What users praise** (App Store reviews and comments, via search [47][48]): "the 3D view makes it easier for the
  kid to build"; a good way to keep track of sets.
- **What users complain about** [47][48]: the battery ran out before the build was finished; phone screens are too
  small; scrolling back to earlier steps is slow; it is laggy; part colours are shown wrong and some steps are wrong;
  the camera controls feel odd; older and small sets are missing. Some parents object to turning a physical toy into
  more screen time. LEGO has been reported as considering phasing out paper instructions, which drew those comments
  [48].
- App Store rating: not confirmed.

### LEGO DUPLO apps

- **LEGO DUPLO World** is for ages 3 to 6. Children build with 3D DUPLO bricks on screen. It supports multi-touch and
  gives parents tips for shared play [50]. Common Sense Media reviews it [51].
- These apps are digital play. They don't guide building with real bricks [50]. DUPLO instructions inside LEGO Builder:
  not confirmed.

### What Tile Builder can take from LEGO

- Rotate and zoom are expected [43]. Young children need big turn buttons, not drags (see `kids-and-ipad.md`).
- Build Together shows that families like building as a team [46]. A one-iPad version would be "you place the
  squares, I place the triangles".
- The complaints lead to clear rules: design for the iPad's big screen, add a quick way to jump to any step, keep
  colour optional (as EverPieces does [3]), work offline, and use little battery.

## 9. Where Tile Builder can be different

### Opportunities (with the evidence)

1. **Show near misses and how to fix them.** Magniko hides what you can't build [7]. EverPieces lists pieces but
   doesn't say what you're missing (not confirmed) [1]. Printables leave counting to the parent [24][35]. A green
   "You can build it!", a "Need 2 more ▲" drawn as tile pictures, and "use short triangles instead" were not found in
   any competitor. This is Tile Builder's clearest gap, as long as the swaps really work.
2. **Each brand's tall triangle, and Connetix extras.** EverPieces uses "common full-size shapes" with colour optional
   [1]. The tall triangle differs by brand (`tiles.md`). Tile Builder's checker validates every project with each
   brand's triangle length. No competitor was found that does this. Projects that need Connetix rectangles, windows or
   doors could be shown only to families who own them.
3. **Children who can't read yet.** Every competitor found is written for a parent to lead: "make with your child"
   [1], "you guide and your little one places the tiles" [8], idea books that need reading [25]. Read-aloud steps,
   piece counts as pictures and big tap targets for a 3- or 4-year-old were not found anywhere. Tile Builder should
   still work well with a parent alongside, since that is clearly how others expect families to use these apps.
4. **Nothing leaves the iPad, and no AI.** EverPieces is already private, but its AI art uploads a photo when chosen
   [1]. Magniko has community sharing [7]. Tilbo has streaks [8]. "No account, no upload, no AI, no streaks" is a
   short, true promise. It is a small difference from EverPieces, not a large one.
5. **One iPad, several children.** EverPieces has one private album and "no profiles" [1] (this probably means no
   social profiles; per-child profiles were not confirmed either way). Tile Builder plans a profile and a "My Builds"
   gallery for each child.
6. **Any browser, no App Store.** EverPieces is iPhone and iPad only [1]. A web app also opens on Android tablets and
   school Chromebooks, and a library or classroom can share a link. Libraries already lend tile kits with printed
   guides [41][42].
7. **Free, with nothing to unlock.** EverPieces charges for three of its four Worlds [1]; its sister app charges $19.99
   [4]. If Tile Builder stays free, the difference is simple. (Price is not decided in `PLAN.md`.)
8. **A guided build, then "now change it".** Connetix and EverPieces both treat guidance as a starting point [2][17].
   Magna-Tiles avoids instructions altogether [11]. An optional prompt after the celebration ("Can you add a tower?")
   would answer parents who prefer open-ended play.
9. **Fix LEGO's complaints from day one.** Use the big iPad screen, a quick way to jump to any step, optional colour,
   offline use and little battery [47][48].

### Threats (be honest)

1. **EverPieces already covers most of the plan:** 3D stages, piece lists, any brand, a private album, no ads and no
   account [1]. It has 100+ builds; Tile Builder's phase 3 aims for 30. EverPieces' web guides already rank in search
   [3]. If it adds an inventory screen, opportunity 1 shrinks to the swaps and the per-brand checks.
2. **Magniko already sells "see what you can build with your tiles"** on iOS and Android, free to download [7].
3. **Tilbo is about to launch** with almost the same pitch (age-matched builds, the tiles you have at home, a photo, a
   sticker) on iPhone, iPad and Mac [8].
4. **Discovery.** Parents look for apps in the App Store, where Tile Builder won't be. Home Screen web apps have
   storage rules to manage (see `kids-and-ipad.md`).
5. **The brands and the books are well established.** Connetix tins come with their own guided designs [17]. The
   PicassoTiles books have 150 to 300+ ideas [24][25]. One printables site has 300 [27]. Thirty hand-made projects will
   look small next to them; the checker and the matching have to make up for it.
6. **Screen-time doubts.** Some parents don't want a physical toy to become screen time [48], and Magna-Tiles promotes
   open play without instructions [11]. Tile Builder's "put the iPad down and build" moments and the absence of streaks
   and points help here.
7. **Hand-made content is slow.** EverPieces and the book authors have a head start. Magniko gets content from its
   community [7]. Tile Builder's choice not to generate projects (D3) keeps quality high but caps volume.

- **Tilbo**: launch date, price, brands, 3D, offline use.
