# What parents and teachers say

User research from public sources for Tile Builder, 2 Oct 2026. It is meant to ground the product brief in parents'
own words. It does not replace talking to families. Section (d) lists what only that can answer.

## How this was gathered, and how far to trust it

- **Only search-result summaries were available.** This session's network proxy blocked every page fetch tried: Reddit,
  Mumsnet's forum pages, DC Urban Moms, the App Store, Google Play, the iTunes API, everpieces.com, Pew and Wikipedia.
  Web search worked, but it returns a summary of each page, not the page itself.
- **No Reddit evidence.** The search tool did not index Reddit (`site:reddit.com` returned no Reddit pages). The forum
  voice below comes from Mumsnet (UK), DC Urban Moms (US), imamother (US) and Slickdeals (US) instead.
- **No app-store reviews.** The session's search budget ran out (the 200-call limit is shared across the session) before
  section 4 of the brief could be researched. EverPieces, LEGO Builder and Brickit reviews are **not covered**. See "Gaps".
- **Labels used on every piece of evidence:**
  - **Quote (search):** words the search tool showed in quotation marks for that page. They have not been checked
    against the page. Check them before using them outside this repo.
  - **Paraphrase:** the search summary's wording of what the page says. Treat it as close, not verbatim.
  - **Vendor:** a company describing its own product. Shows what the market offers, not what parents feel.
  - Where the summary drew on several pages and did not say which one held the point, all the candidate pages are
    listed and the item says "page not pinned".
- **How widespread:** *Strong* means several independent sources say it. *Moderate* means two or three. *Thin* means one
  source, or vendor material only.

## (a) Themes

### 1. Tiles last for years and span a wide age range

**How widespread:** strong. Every forum thread found says some version of this.

- Paraphrase: a parent with a 4-year-old and a 1-year-old calls them one of their most played-with toys. Another says a
  12-year-old and friends still play with them sometimes.
  [Mumsnet: Talk to me about Magnatiles](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5214080-talk-to-me-about-magnatiles)
- Paraphrase: Connetix are the only toy that gets played with practically every day. A child given Magna-Tiles at 3 is
  nearly 7 and still uses them. (Page not pinned: [Mumsnet: Is there a 'proper' brand of magnetic tiles?](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5452221-is-there-a-proper-brand-of-magnetic-tiles),
  [Mumsnet: Magna tiles?](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5237509-magna-tiles),
  [Mumsnet Connetix review](https://www.mumsnet.com/reviews/connetix-tiles-review).)
- Quote (search), an Amazon reviewer quoted by AOL: "My three, four, five and seven-year-old grandsons play with them for
  hours every time they come."
  [AOL: More than 5,000 Amazon shoppers love Magna-Tiles](https://www.aol.com/news/more-5-000-amazon-shoppers-152923931.html)
- Paraphrase: a child got tiles at 2 and still plays at 8½. An 11-year-old still loves them and wants bigger builds.
  Kids aged 5 and 3 play 5 to 6 times a week after 18 months. (Page not pinned:
  [DC Urban Moms: Are Magna Tiles worth it?](https://www.dcurbanmom.com/jforum/posts/list/260788.page) and the AOL
  article above.)

**For Tile Builder:** the library has to grow with a child from 3 to 10. Older kids want *bigger*, not just more.

### 2. "You need more than you think": every family hits the limit of its set

**How widespread:** strong.

- Quote (search): "you need more than you think to make fun things!"
- Quote (search): "We have 100 and it's not enough"
- Quote (search): "200-500 would be amazing"
  (All three: [Mumsnet: How many magnetic tiles](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5193024-how-many-magnetic-tiles))
- Paraphrase: get at least the 100-piece set, because you need a decent number to really build much. Another parent:
  the more tiles the better. [Mumsnet: Talk to me about Magnatiles](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5214080-talk-to-me-about-magnatiles)
- Paraphrase: some customers feel that for the price there aren't enough pieces in the box.
  [The Everymom: An honest review](https://theeverymom.com/magnatiles-review/)

**For Tile Builder:** most families own fewer tiles than the photos they see online need. The "You can build it!"
badge answers the real question: what can *we* make with *ours*? Every age band needs plenty of builds that fit a
32- or 60-piece set.

### 3. Some pieces run out first: triangles and large squares

**How widespread:** moderate.

- Quote (search): "it's important to get triangles as well as squares for more designs".
  [Mumsnet: How many magnetic tiles](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5193024-how-many-magnetic-tiles)
- Quote (search): "Two large squares or even four are not enough for two children". Paraphrase: the children fought
  over the large squares because they are the best for buildings.
  [The Fairy Glitch Mother: Which magnetic tiles are best](https://thefairyglitchmother.com/magnetic-tiles/)
- Paraphrase: the castle towers on the box use only three triangles each, just for show, with no triangle at the back.
  The reviewer's son expected four per tower and ran out of triangles when he built five. (Page not pinned. It was the
  top result for a triangle search: [Galaxus: Magna-Tiles review, "kind of like Lego, but less creative and stable"](https://www.galaxus.fr/en/page/magna-tiles-kind-of-like-lego-but-less-creative-and-stable-33284).)

**For Tile Builder:** "Need 2 more ▲" and the swaps (short triangles for tall, two right triangles for a square) are
aimed at the most common shortfall. The box-photo story shows why every project must be checked by the engine:
a picture that cheats teaches kids the tiles are the problem.

### 4. Siblings: one builds, one knocks down, both want the big squares

**How widespread:** moderate. Few sources, but they are vivid and match theme 3.

- Paraphrase: a mother says she hates magnet tiles. They suit a home with one child, or two kids 10 to 12 years apart.
  Kids play either for the enjoyment of building or for the enjoyment of destroying. Put together, that means
  tears, inappropriate hand use and tiles crashing to the floor, and the tiles go back in the garage.
  [Little Yellow Notepad: What they don't tell you about Magna-Tiles](https://littleyellownotepad.com/2024/04/05/mom-review-what-they-dont-tell-you-about-magna-tiles/)
- Paraphrase: children fought over the large squares, so the parent bought more tiles.
  [The Fairy Glitch Mother](https://thefairyglitchmother.com/magnetic-tiles/)
- The other side, quote (search): kids aged 3, 4, 5 and 7 "play with them for hours" together.
  [AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/more-5-000-amazon-shoppers-152923931.html)

**For Tile Builder:** per-kid profiles are not enough on their own. Worth testing: a build split into a part for each
child, a "my builds" photo taken *before* the knock-down, and projects that are meant to be knocked down (dominoes).

### 5. Age and stage: 3-year-olds lay tiles flat or drift off; it "kicks in" around 4

**How widespread:** moderate.

- Paraphrase: a parent was puzzled when their 3-year-old wasn't interested. Replies: one child built a few towers at 3
  and stopped, then after turning four went crazy building amazing structures. Quote (search): "wait until 4 it will
  kick in". Quote (search): "they're a great toy, but not the holy grail", from a parent whose two kids differed.
  [DC Urban Moms: Anyone's 3 yr old not like Magna Tiles?](https://www.dcurbanmom.com/jforum/posts/list/459482.page)
- Paraphrase: early play is mostly flat, which is normal. Children start with flat patterns or simple houses, then
  lift walls into cubes and towers. Weak magnets make a frustrated child give up. (Page not pinned:
  [Kidsbaron: magnetic tiles for toddlers](https://kidsbaron.com/blog/best-magnetic-tiles-for-toddlers),
  [Kidsbaron: magnetic tiles for 3-year-olds](https://kidsbaron.com/blog/magnetic-tiles-for-3-year-olds). A toy-review
  site, not a parent.)
- Paraphrase: flat 2D animals suit younger kids still developing fine motor skills. Small kids start with simple 3D
  houses and make villages of them. (Page not pinned: [Entertain Your Toddler](https://entertainyourtoddler.com/ways-to-play-with-magnetic-tiles/),
  [Mommy Poppins](https://mommypoppins.com/anywhere-kids/magna-tiles-games-magna-tiles-building-ideas-for-kids).)

**For Tile Builder:** the 3–5 band needs flat picture builds (animals, letters) and tiny 3D builds that stand within
a minute or two. A 3-year-old who isn't interested yet is normal. The app should not make that feel like failure.

### 6. Running out of ideas is a real, paid-for problem

**How widespread:** strong, judged from what families buy and follow rather than from complaints.

- Vendor: PicassoTiles sells a 230-page idea book with 300 builds and a difficulty rating. Paraphrase of reviews:
  an 8-year-old finds it super easy to follow; ideas range from easy to medium difficulty.
  [Amazon: PicassoTiles Idea Book](https://www.amazon.com/PicassoTiles-Idea-Book-90-Structure/dp/B01J4QWM9W)
- Vendor: Panda Mommy Teacher has 723K Instagram followers, says its books have reached 25,000+ families in 70+
  countries, and publishes tutorials with picture diagrams and the number of tiles required.
  [Panda Mommy Teacher](https://pandamommyteacher.com/), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/pandamommyteacher/)
- Vendor: every Connetix product ships with an inspiration book. Connetix also offers free printable challenge cards
  and a "PlayZone" of ideas. [Connetix: Getting started](https://connetixtiles.com/gift-guide/getting-started/),
  [Connetix resource cards](https://connetixtiles.com/resource-cards), [Connetix PlayZone](https://connetixtiles.com/playzone)
- Vendor: Adventure in a Box sells over 300 printable idea cards (letters, numbers, animals, seasonal).
  [Adventure in a Box](https://www.adventure-in-a-box.com/magna-tile-ideas-to-print-and-build/). Teachers Pay Teachers
  has many more ([TPT search](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse?search=magnatiles)).
- Pinterest has many family-made boards of ideas ([Pinterest: Magna Tiles ideas](https://www.pinterest.com/ideas/magna-tiles-ideas/913311779457/)).
  The official Magna-Tiles site and TikTok post builds with the pieces listed ([Magna-Tiles](https://magnatiles.com/)).

**For Tile Builder:** ideas alone are not scarce. What is scarce is ideas that fit *your* tiles and that a child can
follow *without you*. That is where to compete.

### 7. Instructions that a child can't follow alone pull the parent back in

**How widespread:** moderate.

- Quote (search), a parent on a build book: "with written instructions, he has to have a grownup always help him."
  Quote (search): "step by step pictures may have helped more".
- Quote (search), the other side: "the instructions are clear and easy to follow and the pictures are amazing- step by
  step all the way through the designs."
  (Page not pinned: [Amazon: Magnetic Tiles STEM Play Ideas](https://www.amazon.com/Magnetic-Tiles-STEM-Ideas-Hands/dp/B0CSB8BG5Z),
  [Amazon AU](https://www.amazon.com.au/Magnetic-Tiles-STEM-Ideas-Hands/dp/B0D9PPFTS7),
  [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/205059714-magnetic-tiles-stem-play-ideas).)
- Paraphrase, a teacher's tip on printed challenge cards: children must copy the design rather than build on top of
  it, because the cards are not to scale. (Page not pinned: [Teaching Mama: number cards](https://teachingmama.org/magna-tile-number-cards/),
  [Adventure in a Box](https://www.adventure-in-a-box.com/magna-tile-ideas-to-print-and-build/).)
- Paraphrase: children treat idea cards as puzzles. They can invent builds, but copying a card is a different challenge
  that they enjoy. [Adventure in a Box](https://www.adventure-in-a-box.com/magna-tile-ideas-to-print-and-build/)

**For Tile Builder:** this supports picture-first steps, the tiles for each step shown as pictures, and read-aloud.
Life-size tile pictures (so a child can lay a real tile on the screen to match) may be worth testing.

### 8. Magnet strength, collapse and mixing brands

**How widespread:** moderate, and opinions conflict.

- Paraphrase: weak magnets mean structures collapse at the slightest touch, and a frustrated child may give up.
  ([Kidsbaron](https://kidsbaron.com/blog/best-magnetic-tiles-for-toddlers), a review site.)
- Paraphrase, forum: the original brand has stronger magnets; some have heard magnets come out of other brands' tiles,
  which would be a HUGE choking hazard. [imamother: Question regarding magnet tiles](https://www.imamother.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=306443)
- Paraphrase: cheap Amazon sets are OK but the magnets aren't as strong. Connetix and Magna-Tiles work together,
  although they may not always sit flush. (Page not pinned: [Mumsnet Connetix review](https://www.mumsnet.com/reviews/connetix-tiles-review),
  [Mumsnet: Magnatiles, are they a really good toy?](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/_chat/5442820-magnatiles-are-they-a-really-good-toy),
  [Mumsnet: Best magnet tiles please?](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5199194-best-magnet-tiles-please).)
- Paraphrase, the opposite view after two years: no meaningful difference in magnet strength; Magna-Tiles, PicassoTiles
  and Tytan mix together fine. [Kevin Lu: Is Magna-Tiles worth it?](https://kevin-lu.com/blog/is-magne-tiles-worth-it-our-honest-answer-after-2-years/)
- Paraphrase, a tester: Magna-Tiles sit flush; cheaper sets showed slight gaps.
  [ScienceBasedKids](https://sciencebasedkids.com/reviews/magna-tiles-100/)
- Paraphrase, deal-forum comments: OG Magna-Tile quality is unbeatable; only Magna-Tiles, Connetix and PlayMags
  have metal rivets. (Page not pinned: [Slickdeals 17943825](https://slickdeals.net/f/17943825),
  [Slickdeals 18717457](https://slickdeals.net/f/18717457).)

**For Tile Builder:** many families own a mix (they topped up with a cheaper brand). Steps should say how to keep a
build standing (base first, close the ring, then go up). Tall builds don't belong in the 3–5 band. The "best with one
brand" note on large closed loops in `tiles.md` matches what parents report.

### 9. Tiles are bought partly as the screen-free toy, so an app is in tension

**How widespread:** strong for screen-time worry; moderate for tiles being framed as the alternative. Few direct
parent quotes found on this.

- Survey: 71% of US parents of a child under 12 are at least somewhat worried their child might spend too much time in
  front of screens; 31% are very worried. 80% say their child aged 5–11 ever uses a tablet.
  [Pew Research, 2020](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2020/07/28/parenting-children-in-the-age-of-screens/)
- Vendor: the best-known tile creator describes her work as simple, screen-free play ideas.
  [Panda Mommy Teacher](https://pandamommyteacher.com/)
- Press framing: [TODAY: "No iPads Here: These Are the Screen-Free Toys Parents Swear By"](https://www.today.com/shop/best-screen-free-toys-rcna243972).
- Paraphrase, occupational therapy: open-ended tile building grows planning and spatial reasoning without a single
  instruction sheet; therapists watch what a child builds when nobody hands them instructions. (Page not pinned:
  [Createon: 3 ways to use magnetic tiles in OT](https://www.createon.com/resources/3-ways-to-use-magnetic-tiles-in-occupational-therapy.htm),
  [Createon: therapeutic benefits](https://www.createon.com/blog/the-therapeutic-benefits-of-magnetic-tiles-for-child-development.htm).)

**For Tile Builder:** the app has to earn its place by getting kids off the screen and onto the floor fast. Short
time on screen per build, and "make it your own" endings after a guided build, are worth more than more screens.
Taking a photo of a child's *own* design (not only of library builds) keeps free building at the centre.

### 10. Teachers and therapists already use tiles with picture cards

**How widespread:** moderate.

- Vendor: Magna-Tiles says it was invented by an educator for educators and born in the classroom in 1997.
  [Magna-Tiles](https://magnatiles.com/blogs/news/the-power-of-meaningful-play-with-magna-tiles-sets)
- Paraphrase: teachers print, laminate and reuse challenge cards at math and literacy centres; students copy a picture
  with their tiles. (Page not pinned: [Teaching Mama](https://teachingmama.org/magna-tile-number-cards/),
  [TPT task cards](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse/free?search=magna+tile+task+cards).)
- Paraphrase: therapists use tiles for fine motor skills, colour and shape recognition, spatial words and motor
  planning. [Connect the Dots Pediatric Therapy](https://ctdpediatrictherapy.com/magna-tiles-therapy-toys/),
  [Speech Room News: magnetic tiles in speech therapy](https://thespeechroomnews.com/2024/01/magnetic-tiles-in-speech-therapy.html),
  [The Inspired Treehouse](https://theinspiredtreehouse.com/the-best-magnatile-ideas-for-kids/),
  [Integrated Learning Strategies](https://ilslearningcorner.com/2016-03-magna-tiles-for-hand-eye-coordination-problem-solving-sensory-issues-motor-planning/)

**For Tile Builder:** a likely second audience. A classroom iPad is shared, and a class set is bigger than a family's.
No accounts and data on the device suit schools. Not a launch goal, but worth one question in interviews.

### 11. Ads, in-app purchases and subscriptions are what parents hate most in kids' apps

**How widespread:** strong.

- Study: 95% of commonly downloaded apps for children 5 and under had at least one kind of advertising. Quote (search),
  an example of an in-app prompt: "You can play with these cute tiny animals for a small fee. Just ask your parents".
  (Page not pinned: [Michigan Medicine, 2018](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/advertising-kids-apps-more-prevalent-parents-may-realize),
  [Michigan Medicine, 2022](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use).)
- Quote (search), an analysis of 1-star reviews: ads and purchase prompts are "the single most emotional complaint
  category" for kids' apps. Paraphrase: they are about 35% of 1-star reviews in the kids category. Subscription
  complaints centre on trials that charged early, yearly renewals and cancellations that would not go through. Thin
  content is another pattern.
  [Unstar: Kids and parenting app complaints, 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/kids-parenting-app-reviews-what-parents-complain-about-2026)
- Survey: 67.1% of US parents worry their kids overspend in mobile games; 40.8% say their child made a purchase
  without their knowledge. [SellCell](https://www.sellcell.com/blog/more-than-two-thirds-of-parents-worry-kids-overspending-on-in-app-purchases/)
- Quote (search), App Store review praising the opposite: "no ads or purchases WOW" (an emoji is left out).
  [Khan Academy Kids on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217)

**For Tile Builder:** "No ads. Nothing to buy. Nothing leaves this iPad." belongs on the first screen a parent sees.
"Need 2 more ▲" must never read as "go buy more tiles". It should lead to a swap or a different build.

### 12. Parents want to play along sometimes, but often can't

**How widespread:** moderate (research); thin (direct quotes).

- Research: 21% of parents of children 0–8 say they use a tablet together with their child "all or most of the time";
  another 42% "some of the time". [Frontiers in Psychology, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.576940/xml)
- Paraphrase: studies of co-play often assume parents have the time, knowledge and experience, yet parents are short of
  time; co-using devices can be emotionally charged for parent and child. (Page not pinned:
  [YouthCare, arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02151),
  [ScienceDirect: joint engagement with touchscreens](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016363832100062X).)
- The book review in theme 7 ("he has to have a grownup always help him") shows co-play forced by the design,
  not chosen.

**For Tile Builder:** design for both. A child of 4 can follow a build alone; a parent who sits down has something to
do (hand over tiles, read the step, turn the model). Privacy evidence was thin in this search. The best signal is
that the closest competitor sells it: EverPieces stresses private progress and no account (see Gaps).

## (b) Jobs to be done

Each line says which theme it comes from. "Inferred" means the evidence points there but nobody said it outright.

### Parents

1. When my child says "what can I build?", I want to hand them a build that works with the tiles we already own, so
   they play for a while without me and without a show. (themes 2, 6, 7)
2. When I let my child use the iPad, I want it to lead back to hands-on play, with no ads or purchases, so I don't feel
   guilty about it. (9, 11)
3. When the kids fight over the tiles, I want a fair way to share them or build together, so play doesn't end in tears.
   (4)
4. When my child is ready for more, I want harder builds that match their age, so the tiles we paid for keep being
   used for years. (1, 5)
5. When I do sit down to play, I want something we can build together that my child couldn't manage alone. (12)
6. When we're thinking about getting more tiles, I want to know which pieces would open up the most builds. (2, 3;
   inferred. It sits close to "pester power", so handle with care.)
7. When my child makes something they're proud of, I want to keep it before it's knocked down or tidied away. (4;
   inferred)

### Kids 3–5

1. When I want to make "a real thing", I want to see the picture and the next tile, so I can do it myself without
   reading. (5, 7)
2. I want it to stand up quickly, so I feel I did it. (5, 8)
3. I want to make flat pictures (an animal, my letter) as well as little houses. (5)

### Kids 6–8

1. When I see a cool build (a castle, a rocket, a garage with ramps), I want to make it with our tiles and have it not
   fall down. (3, 6, 8; ramps and garages from [Mumsnet threads](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/christmas/5452221-is-there-a-proper-brand-of-magnetic-tiles), page not pinned)
2. I want a challenge, like copying a picture exactly. (7)
3. I want to play inside what I built with my cars, animals and figures. (5, 6)

### Kids 9–10

Evidence for this band is thin: an 11-year-old who wants bigger builds and a 12-year-old who still plays (theme 1).

1. I want big, hard builds that use most of our tiles. (1, 2)
2. I want to design my own and show it off. (9; inferred)
3. I want to help my little brother or sister build. (4, 12; inferred)

## (c) Proto-personas

These are families, not demographic types. Each detail is tied to the evidence above.

### The shared bin: two kids, one set

- Kids about 4 and 7. One 100-piece set, later topped up with a cheaper brand (themes 2, 8).
- The large squares and triangles run out first. The younger one knocks down what the older one builds (3, 4).
- The parent wants 20 calm minutes while making dinner and doesn't want that to be a show (9).
- Ideas come from the box booklet and the odd Pinterest pin; the older child can't always follow them alone (6, 7).
- **Wins if:** each child gets a build that fits the tiles they have, and there is a way to share without a fight.
- **Loses if:** the app shows builds they can't make, or it becomes another screen to argue over.

### The first set: one preschooler, a gift box

- One child of 3. A 32- or 60-piece set from a relative (themes 2, 5).
- Mostly flat building, a few towers, then interest fades. The parent wonders if it's "worth it" and whether they need
  more tiles (2, 5).
- The parent is happy to sit and build together but doesn't know what to make (6, 12).
- **Wins if:** there are lots of tiny, flat and two-minute builds, read aloud, that make the child feel able.
- **Loses if:** builds need 100 tiles, or the app feels like a test.

### The long-haul builders: years of tiles, older kids

- Kids about 6 and 9. Several hundred tiles across brands, collected over years (themes 1, 2, 8).
- The older one has outgrown simple ideas and wants big projects; the family follows tile creators online (1, 6).
- A grown-up joins at weekends for a big build (12).
- **Wins if:** there are hard builds that use what they own, mixed brands are handled, and the older child can design
  and photograph their own.
- **Loses if:** the library feels babyish or entering several hundred tiles takes too long.

A school or therapy setting (theme 10) is a possible fourth user. It is not a launch persona.

## (d) Open questions for talking to families

1. **Will a parent enter their tiles?** How long will they spend? Do they know which sets they bought? Would "pick your
   set, then adjust" be enough, or would they rather count? No public evidence was found on this.
2. How many tiles, of which brands, do families really own? How mixed are they? Do they know their tall-triangle type?
3. Do kids want to follow steps at all? How much of their tile time would be guided, and how much free? Does a guided
   build lead to free building afterwards, or replace it?
4. Can a 3- or 4-year-old turn a 3D model on a screen into real tiles? Do the turn buttons help or confuse?
5. Where is the iPad during a build (floor, table, a parent's hand)? Who holds it? Do tiles end up on the screen?
6. Does the parent stay or step away? How long is a typical session?
7. How do siblings share one iPad and one bin? Would a build split into parts for two children help?
8. Do parents already photograph builds? Where do the photos go (family chat, grandparents)? Is "photos stay on the
   iPad" a plus or a limit?
9. Is an app for a screen-free toy acceptable to the parents who bought tiles for that reason? What wording makes it
   feel OK?
10. Does "Need 2 more ▲" make kids ask for more tiles? Do parents mind?
11. Do kids care about matching colours exactly, or only shapes?
12. What would parents pay, if anything: free, one price, or nothing because it's a hobby project? (They do pay for
    idea books, theme 6.)
13. Is the read-aloud voice welcome? Which languages do families need?
14. Would a teacher or therapist use it on a shared iPad with a class set?

## Gaps: what this research did not cover

These were in the brief but could not be researched in this session. Fill them before the brief is final.

- **App-store reviews (brief item 4): not covered.** Nothing was gathered for EverPieces, LEGO Builder or Brickit.
  Suggested searches: "EverPieces app review", "LEGO Builder app review zoom rotate", "LEGO Builder app offline",
  "Brickit app review subscription", "Brickit app accuracy pile".
- **EverPieces, the closest competitor, from its own site only (vendor).** 100+ guided 3D builds, 30+ free; no ads and
  no subscription; extra "Worlds" are one-time purchases; brand-independent; each model shows the shapes and quantities
  needed and colours don't have to match; finishing a build needs no photo, account or AI; optional AI art uploads a
  temporary photo copy after the parent agrees; per-build web pages such as a carrot.
