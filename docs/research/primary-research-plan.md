# Primary research plan: talking to families

Desk research tells us what is known in general. These sessions answer what only real families can: will a parent
really count their tiles, can a 4-year-old follow a picture of a build, and what makes a kid want to build something.
The findings feed the brief before any design work starts.

## The questions

| # | Question | Why it matters | How we find out |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Will a parent enter an inventory, and how long does it take? | "You can build it" depends on it | Inventory exercise |
| R2 | Do parents know their brand and set, or only "a big tub of tiles"? | Decides whether set presets help | Interview, inventory exercise |
| R3 | Can a 3-, 4- and 5-year-old follow a picture of a build? How many tiles per step before they get lost? | Sets the step size per age | Build session with paper cards |
| R4 | Does a 3D model the kid can turn help, or confuse them? | Decides whether build mode is 3D for young kids or a fixed picture | Build session with the castle prototype on an iPad |
| R5 | Where does the screen sit while they build, and does the kid look up from the tiles to it? | The app must hand off to the tiles, not hold attention | Build session |
| R6 | What happens when a build collapses or they run out of a piece? | Shapes the "need 2 more" and swap design | Build session |
| R7 | Do kids follow a build to the end, change it, or abandon it for their own idea? | Instructions versus open play | Build session |
| R8 | Which projects do kids pick, and why? | The library's themes and the gallery's look | Picture sort |
| R9 | Who is there: one kid, siblings of different ages, a parent building too? | Profiles, co-play, and difficulty | Interview, build session |
| R10 | What would make a parent trust this on their kid's iPad, and what would make them delete it? | Privacy, ads, screen time | Interview |

## Who

- **Jordan's family first.** Run every session once at home to fix the materials before involving anyone else.
- **Then 5 to 6 more families**, covering:
  - every age band: at least two kids aged 3–5, two aged 6–8 and two aged 9–10
  - at least three brands between them, and at least one family that mixes brands
  - at least two families with siblings of different ages
  - girls and boys in every age band
- **Optional:** one preschool teacher or occupational therapist who uses tiles with groups.

## Sessions

Each family: about 60 minutes at home, with the family's own tiles. One person runs it and one takes notes.

### 1. Parent interview (15 minutes)

1. Tell me about the tiles. When did you get them, which ones, and where do they live?
2. When did someone last play with them? What happened?
3. What do the kids usually build? Does anyone ever say "I don't know what to build"? What do you do then?
4. Where do build ideas come from today (the box booklet, Pinterest, YouTube, friends, school)? Walk me through the last time.
5. Do the kids build alone, together, or with you? What is that like with different ages?
6. Which apps do your kids use? Which ones do you like and which do you avoid, and why?
7. What would make you delete a kids' app?
8. If an app could tell you "you have enough tiles to build this", how much effort would you put into telling it what you have?

### 2. Inventory exercise (10 minutes)

Hand the parent a printed sheet with a picture of each shape (square, large square, three kinds of triangle, and the
Connetix extras). Ask them to count their tiles by shape. Time it. Note:

- Do they know their brand and set?
- Do they sort by shape, or give up and estimate?
- Do the kids help count?
- How long does it take, and do they say it was worth it?

### 3. Build session (25 minutes per kid)

Use printed idea cards, made from projects we draw in our own style: a picture of the finished build, the tiles
needed shown as pictures, then numbered steps. Each card is printed in two step sizes, either 1–2 tiles a step or 4–6
tiles a step.

| Age | Card 1 | Card 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | A flat picture (a house or a fish, lying on the table, about 8 tiles) | A small 3D box with a roof (about 10 tiles) |
| 6–8 | A small house (about 20 tiles) | A rocket or tower (about 30 tiles) |
| 9–10 | The castle (90 tiles, from the prototype) | Free choice from 6 cards |

Then 5 minutes with the castle prototype on an iPad. Watch whether the kid presses Next, turns the model, and where
they put the iPad.

Watch for, and note with the time:
- where the card or iPad sits, and how often the kid looks at it
- which step they get stuck on, and why
- what they say out loud, especially shape words
- whether they ask an adult for help, and for what
- if anything collapses, and what happens next
- whether they finish, change the design, or switch to their own idea, and when
- how they feel at the end, and whether they want to show someone or photograph it

Don't help unless the kid asks or is upset. If they ask, give the smallest hint you can and note it.

### 4. Picture sort (10 minutes, kids)

Lay out 20 printed project pictures across all themes. Ask: "Which ones would you like to build? Put them on this
side." Then: "Which one first?" Note the reasons they give, and whether brothers and sisters pick differently.

## Consent and care

- Ask the parent for consent in writing, and ask each kid out loud if they want to play. A kid can stop at any time.
- No video of faces. Photos only of hands and builds, and only with the parent's OK.
- Notes use first names or a code, never surnames. They stay out of this repo; only the summary is committed.
- Thank each family with something for their kids (for example a set of tiles).

## After the sessions

1. Write each session up the same day on the notes template: what happened, quotes, and the time of each event.
2. Group the observations by question R1–R10 and write what we learned, with how many families each finding came
   from.
3. Update the brief (`PRODUCT.md`) with what changed, and list anything still unknown.

## Materials to make before the first session

- Inventory counting sheet (one page, shape pictures, a space for each count)
- 8 idea cards in two step sizes, drawn in a neutral style so we are testing the format, not the look
- 20 project pictures for the sort
- Notes template and consent form
- The castle prototype on an iPad
